# 04 — LLM & Agentic Differential-Diagnosis SOTA (2023–2026)

**Scope:** S4 of the public-release research program. LLM diagnostic accuracy on vignettes, LLMs as symptom checkers/triage, multi-agent medical frameworks (direct prior art for AegisMed), RAG over guidelines, interactive/active diagnosis (prior-art landscape for DEMAT), hallucination/safety failure modes.
**Compiled:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence protocol:** README.md rules (≥3 independent sources per load-bearing claim; verbatim quotes with URL + publisher + date; `WEAK-EVIDENCE` and `UNKNOWN` markers where the standard is not met).
**Note on method:** most journal pages (Nature, JAMA, OUP, medRxiv) block direct fetches; where a verbatim number below comes from a search-result snippet of the primary page rather than a completed fetch, that is flagged inline. Fetching the flagged primaries is listed under `UNKNOWN` at the end.

## TL;DR

1. **Vignette benchmarks are saturating while realistic ones are not.** MedQA (USMLE-style) scores climbed from ~86% (GPT-4, Med-PaLM 2, 2023) to ~96% (o-series, 2024–2025), but OpenAI's own realistic HealthBench still had o3 at only 60% in May 2025 (GPT-3.5 Turbo: 16%) and HealthBench Hard topped out at 32% [4,5,17].
2. **Sequential/agentic formats destroy static-benchmark accuracy.** AgentClinic: solving MedQA in a sequential, information-seeking clinical environment produces "diagnostic accuracies that can drop to below a tenth of the original accuracy" [8]. AegisMed's 98% top-1 on 200 self-referential synthetic cases is exactly the kind of number this literature predicts will not transfer.
3. **Adaptive multi-agent routing (MDAgents) is the direct, published prior art for AegisMed's orchestration** — it assigns solo-vs-team collaboration based on estimated case complexity and won 7/10 benchmarks with "a significant improvement of up to 4.2% (p < 0.05)" [9]. MedicalAgentsBench (862 complex questions) shows agent scaffolds and reasoning models are complementary: "the highest accuracy is achieved by layering agent workflows onto an internalized reasoning model (i.e., o3-mini + MDAgents with 35.1%)" [10].
4. **Interactive diagnosis — DEMAT's claimed novelty — is a crowded lane.** Google's AMIE optimizes information-seeking diagnostic dialogue and beat PCPs on 28 of 32 axes [12]; the follow-up Nature paper (302 NEJM CPC cases) reports AMIE top-1 29.2% / top-10 59.1% and clinician top-10 rising from 33.6% to 51.8% with AMIE assistance [13]; the Q4Dx benchmark (Sci Rep, Jan 2026) formalizes "diagnostic questioning efficiency" and explicitly notes information-gain metrics "have not yet been widely adopted in medicine" [14]. **DEMAT's patent defensible core is narrower than "active questioning": entropy-driven selection over a fixed condition graph with deterministic rule-based extraction, and triage-level (not DDx-level) output.**
5. **Triage is where LLMs are weakest relative to humans.** EUSEM 2025: ChatGPT-3.5 triage accuracy 50.4% vs nurses 65.5% and doctors 70.6%; AI sensitivity for urgent cases 58.3% [15]. A 2026 Nature Medicine stress test (secondary coverage) reports ChatGPT Health "under-triaged" ~51.6% of gold-standard emergencies [16]. Red-flag sensitivity — not top-1 accuracy — is the binding safety constraint.
6. **Guideline-grounded RAG measurably helps** (Almanac: +18% average over vanilla ChatGPT across specialties; cardiology 91% vs 69%) and measurably reduces fabrication (vanilla ChatGPT: 31% of nephrology references "completely fabricated") [19]. Hallucination in medical summarization is 1.47% with a rigorous framework (but 3.45% omissions) [20]; other multi-model audits report 50–82% hallucination rates [21]. Grounding, structured output and rule-based fallback are the accepted mitigations.
7. **Multi-agent debate is not a free win:** "multi-agent debating systems, in their current form, do not reliably outperform other proposed prompting strategies" and are "more sensitive to different hyperparameter settings and difficult to optimize" [11].

## 1. LLM diagnostic accuracy on clinical vignettes (MedQA and beyond)

**2023 frontier (GPT-4 / Med-PaLM 2).**

- Med-PaLM 2 reported "86.5% accuracy on USMLE-style questions in the MedQA" (reported via alphaXiv aggregator page for arXiv:2305.09617, "Towards Expert-Level Medical Question Answering with Fast and Efficient Retrieval…"/Med-PaLM 2 line; snippet only). `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (paper page not fetched; number consistent across two secondary aggregators).
- GPT-4 ≈ 86.2% on MedQA per the OpenAI GPT-4 Technical Report (2023). `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (secondary summaries only).
- Kanjee, Crowe & Rodman, *JAMA* 2023;330(4):317-318, "Accuracy of a Generative AI Model in a Complex Diagnostic Challenge": GPT-4 on 70 NEJM clinicopathological conference (CPC) cases — correct diagnosis ranked **first in ~39%** of cases and **included in the differential in ~64%**. JAMA and PubMed pages returned cookie walls; numbers captured from secondary academic citations in search results. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (primary quote unavailable) — but the same 70-case set is re-used with GPT-4 numbers inside the McDuff *Nature* 2025 paper [13], which states "For n = 1, GPT-4 performs marginally better but not statistically significantly" (vs AMIE's 29.2% top-1 on that subset), consistent with a top-1 in the high-20s/30s rather than anything above ~40%.

**2023–2024 human-comparison vignette studies.**

- Goh et al., "Use of GPT-4 to Diagnose Complex Clinical Cases," *JAMA Network Open* 2024 (NEJM Image Challenge cases): GPT-4 "correctly diagnosed an average of 21.8 cases (57%)" vs "medical-journal readers correctly diagnosed an average of 13.7 cases (36%)" (Digital Health Wire, 2023-12-04, secondary). A second secondary source (Mondaq, 2023-11-30) reports 52.7% vs 36% and "outperforming 99.98% of simulated human readers." The two secondaries disagree on 57% vs 52.7% — treat the exact figure as `UNKNOWN` until the JAMA Netw Open page is fetched. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (2 independent secondaries, conflicting decimals).

**2024–2026 reasoning models (o-series, DeepSeek-R1).**

- o-series on MedQA: "OpenAI o1 (Dec 17, 2024, high reasoning mode) leads the leaderboard at ~96.5% accuracy on MedQA" (benchmarklist.com aggregator, fetched via search 2026-10-27); o1-preview reported at "~91.1% accuracy on USMLE-style questions" in early coverage (Reddit/r/singularity citing a benchmark table). `WEAK-EVIDENCE` — both are aggregators; no primary OpenAI/academic page fetched.
- DeepSeek-R1: Moëll et al., "An In-Depth Analysis of DeepSeek R1" (arXiv:2504.00016, 2025-03-27; also PubMed 40607450): "DeepSeek R1 achieves 93% diagnostic accuracy and shows patterns of medical reasoning" (verbatim from arXiv HTML snippet), with "Analysis of the seven error cases" (i.e., ~93% ≈ 130/140 or 7 errors on the analyzed set — exact N `UNKNOWN`). Independent corroboration: a *Scientific Reports* 2025 study (doi:10.1038/s41598-025-31010-4, via sciety.org snippet) "DeepSeek reached 93% accuracy on Step 2 CK, outperforming ChatGPT, Grok, and Qwen"; plus a medRxiv preprint (2025-02-06, doi:10.1101/2025.02.06.25321749) dedicated to R1 on the USMLE. **3 independent sources — claim stands: R1 ≈ 93% on USMLE-style items.**
- OpenAI HealthBench (Arora et al., arXiv:2505.08775, 2025-05-13; fetched via arXiv API): "an open-source benchmark measuring the performance and safety of large language models in healthcare" with **5,000 multi-turn conversations**, "conversation-specific rubrics created by 262 physicians," "48,562 unique rubric criteria." Scores: **GPT-3.5 Turbo 16%, GPT-4o 32%, o3 60%**; HealthBench **Hard: top score 32%**. This is the single best "realistic conversation" datapoint in the public record — note it is conversation quality, not diagnosis ranking.
- Open-weights medical models: Med42-v2 (M42, Llama-3-based) reported at "~90%+ accuracy on MedQA (USMLE)" and MedGemma 27B at 87.7% MedQA (both via secondary aggregator/search summaries). `WEAK-EVIDENCE`. Relevant to AegisMed only as a feasibility datapoint: open/free models on OpenRouter are plausibly in the high-80s-to-90s on MedQA-style MCQ but that number says little about symptom-based triage (§2).

**2023→2026 shift:** exam-style accuracy went from "passes the bar" (2023) to saturated 93–96% (2025–2026) *for frontier models*; the field's own follow-ups (HealthBench, AgentClinic, MedicalAgentsBench) show that realistic and sequential medical performance is 30–60% — i.e., the hard problem moved from knowledge recall to information-seeking, abstention and safety.

## 2. LLM as symptom checker / triage (lay symptom descriptions → diagnosis + disposition)

- **Head-to-head vs dedicated symptom checkers.** The strongest comparison literature remains vignette-based: classical symptom checkers top out with top-1 diagnosis accuracy "ranging from 5% to 50% across tools" on 45 vignettes over 23 tools (Avey evaluation, Hammoud et al., CMU-Q 2023 PDF); LLM-based tools generally beat rule-based symptom checkers in the 2024–2026 comparisons, but the specific JAMA Netw Open / Lancet Digital Health head-to-head with layperson phrasing that the scope asked for could not be pinned to a primary page this pass. `UNKNOWN — how to find out: PubMed search "large language model" AND "symptom checker" 2023-2026, fetch JAMA Netw Open 2024;7:e2354124-adjacent records and Lancet Digit Health 2024 triage comparison; check NEJM AI 2024-2026 triage papers.`
- **AI vs nurse/doctor triage (EUSEM, Sept 2025 press release; fetched):** "AI's overall accuracy was 50.4%, compared to 65.5% for nurses and 70.6% for doctors." Sensitivity: "for AI was also lower at 58.3% compared to nurses, who scored 73.8%, and doctors, who scored 83.0%." Method per the release: 44 nurses + 6 doctors at Vilnius University Hospital Santaros Klinikos triaged cases "from 110 reports cited in the PubMed database" using "the Manchester Triage System"; the AI arm was "ChatGPT (version 3.5)." Caveats are in the release itself (small, single-centre, GPT-3.5). Publisher: European Society for Emergency Medicine (eusem.org), 2025-09.
- **LLM ED-triage on real patients:** Zaboli et al. 2025, "Chat-GPT in triage: Still far from surpassing human expertise," retrospective study of 2,658 ED patients (ChatGPT-4.0): "LLMs have limited utility in ED triage, particularly due to low sensitivity for high-risk patients, leading to under-triage" (secondary capture via ResearchGate/Google Scholar snippet). `WEAK-EVIDENCE`.
- **Deployed consumer LLM triage stress test:** Ramaswamy et al., *Nature Medicine* (Feb 2026 per secondary coverage), "ChatGPT Health performance in a structured test of triage recommendations": ChatGPT Health "under-triaged 52% (51.6%) of cases" among gold-standard emergencies. Captured from three independent secondary outlets (JR Report 2026-03-05; Epocrates clinical takeaway 2026-03-05; ASCO AI 2026-03-18). `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (no primary fetch; number consistent across outlets). A March 2026 arXiv rebuttal ("Evaluation format, not model capability, drives triage failure") contests the framing — read both before citing in the paper.
- **AI triage decision support that worked (as decision support):** Taylor, *NEJM AI* 2025 (doi:10.1056/AIoa2400296): "Implementation of an AI-Based Triage Decision Support" was "associated with improved triage performance and ED patient flow" (snippet). Consistent with the EUSEM conclusion: "it could serve as a decision-support tool in specific clinical contexts and in overwhelmed emergency departments. However … careful integration and human oversight are crucial."
- **Baseline symptom-checker context (pre-LLM):** Riboli-Sasco et al., medRxiv 2021-12-21 (Imperial College): triage accuracy of digital symptom checkers "ranged from 48.8% to 90.1%" with "significant under-triage of emergency cases." Useful as the historical bar AegisMed is compared against.

## 3. Agentic multi-agent medical frameworks (direct prior art for AegisMed)

**MDAgents — NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv:2404.15155; fetched).** Yubin Kim et al. (MIT Media Lab). Core mechanism: "automatically assigning a collaboration structure to a team of LLMs" — "The assigned solo or group collaboration structure is tailored to the medical task at hand," mimicking real-world solo-physician-vs-team routing. Results (verbatim): "MDAgents achieved the best performance in seven out of ten benchmarks", "showing a significant improvement of up to 4.2% (p < 0.05) compared to previous methods' best performances", and vs a single agent it "resulted in an average accuracy improvement of 11.8%." Dates: v1 2024-04-22, v3 2024-10-30. **This is the closest published analogue to AegisMed's agent-availability/agreement blending and must be cited and differentiated in both the patent and CHIL paper.**

**MedAgents — Findings of ACL 2024 (arXiv:2311.10537; fetched).** Tang, Zou, … Gerstein. "MedAgents, a novel multi-disciplinary collaboration framework for the medical domain"; "MedAgents leverages LLM-based agents in a role-playing setting that participate in a collaborative multi-round discussion"; "This training-free framework encompasses five critical steps: gathering domain experts, proposing individual analyses, summarising …"; "Experimental results on nine datasets (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, and six subtasks from MMLU) establish that our proposed MedAgents" (percentages in full text, not abstract; exact figures `UNKNOWN — how to find out: fetch ACL Anthology 2024.findings-acl.599 PDF`). Zero-shot, training-free.

**AgentClinic (arXiv:2405.07960; fetched).** Schmidgall et al. A "multimodal agent benchmark" evaluating LLMs in simulated sequential clinical environments spanning "nine medical specialties and seven languages." The load-bearing finding for any diagnosis agent: models that ace MedQA produce "diagnostic accuracies that can drop to below a tenth of the original accuracy" when MedQA is "solved in the sequential decision-making format" of AgentClinic-MedQA. Also: "agents from Claude-3.5 outperformed other backbones in most settings" and "Llama-3 shows up to 92% relative improvements with the notebook tool" (tool use matters). **This paper is the template for a non-circular evaluation of AegisMed.**

**MedicalAgentsBench (arXiv:2503.07459; fetched).** Shao, Tang, … Gerstein. "a filtered benchmark of 862 complex clinical questions" from "the union of eight medical datasets via difficulty-aware curation and contamination screening." Evaluated "three internalized reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, o1-mini, and o3-mini), seven base models, and nine externalized agent-based methods." Key results: "internalized and externalized approaches each independently improve performance, and that their benefits compound"; "the highest accuracy is achieved by layering agent workflows onto an internalized reasoning model (i.e., o3-mini + MDAgents with 35.1%)"; and this combination "dominates the cost-performance frontier." Note what 35.1% means: **on genuinely hard, contamination-screened medical reasoning, the best 2025 agent+reasoning system gets ~1 in 3 — a good calibration for what "advanced" means.**

**Multi-agent debate — mostly a negative result (arXiv:2311.17371, ICML 2024 version "Should we be going MAD?"; fetched).** Smit et al. (InstaDeep): "multi-agent debate (MAD) has emerged as a promising strategy for enhancing the truthfulness of LLMs" but "multi-agent debating systems, in their current form, do not reliably outperform other proposed prompting strategies" (i.e., self-consistency/multi-path ensembling), and "they are more sensitive to different hyperparameter settings and difficult to optimize." Caveat from the same abstract: tuned MAD (e.g., Multi-Persona) can win, and "adjusting agent agreement levels … can significantly enhance performance and even surpass all other non-debate" protocols. **Implication: AegisMed's "agent agreement" signal should be built on independent, evidence-anchored runs (or conformal calibration, see 05), not on debate rounds.**

**Test-time compute for medical reasoning (2025–2026).** The MedicalAgentsBench comparison above is the cleanest public evidence: reasoning models (o1-mini/o3-mini/DeepSeek-R1) help, and agent scaffolds compound. HealthBench corroborates directionally (o3 60% vs GPT-4o 32%). `UNKNOWN — how to find out: dedicated test-time-scaling medical studies (e.g., arXiv 2025-2026 "test-time compute clinical reasoning") were not individually fetched this pass.`

**Follow-ups 2025–2026 (coverage note).** The lane is now crowded: MedAgentBench (virtual EHR environment, arXiv:2501.14654), MedAgentBoard (NeurIPS 2025), MedAgentGym (training env), adaptive-collaboration demo paper (arXiv:2411.00248), MED-MAD / DMoA structured-debate variants, and an OpenReview paper "Examining the Vulnerability of Multi-Agent Medical Frameworks" (sequential context degrades decision quality). AegisMed's differentiation must be argued against this set, not just MDAgents.

## 4. Retrieval-augmented & guideline-grounded diagnosis

**Measured improvements from grounding in guidelines (Miao et al., *Medicina* 2024;60(3):445; PMC10972059; fetched).**

- The paper showcases "a specialized ChatGPT model integrated with a RAG system, tailored to align with the KDIGO 2023 guidelines for chronic kidney disease."
- Parametric-knowledge failure quantified: "Our analysis of 610 nephrology-related references showed that only 62% of ChatGPT's references existed. Meanwhile, 31% were completely fabricated, and 7% were partial or incomplete."
- RAG lift (citing the Almanac system): "Almanac outperformed ChatGPT by an average of 18% across various medical specialties. The most notable improvement was seen in cardiology, where Almanac achieved 91% accuracy compared to ChatGPT's 69%."
- Structured guideline extraction works at expert level: "RECTIFIER exhibited a sensitivity of 92% and a specificity of 94%, whereas the study staff recorded a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 84%."
- Bottom line in the paper: "the customized GPT-4 model enhanced with a RAG system provides responses that are more specific, detailed, and nuanced" and "are found to be in close alignment with the KDIGO 2023 CKD guidelines."

**Corroborating landscape (secondary, but consistent):** Nanua et al. 2025 (PMC12616094, "cited 25 times") frames RAG as "grounding LLM outputs in dynamically retrieved source documents"; a 2025 Springer evaluation of RAG grounded in consensus-developed clinical practice guidelines reports improved diagnostic/treatment recommendations; RAG² (arXiv:2411.00300) adds rationale-guided retrieval for medical QA. CONFLARE (arXiv:2404.04287) applies conformal methods to retrieval uncertainty in RAG — directly relevant to AegisMed's FAISS layer (see 05).

**Evidence-bound report generation.** The McDuff/AMIE line [13] is the strongest template for AegisMed's report agent: produce a ranked DDx list with explicit top-k recall, not a single answer; and report appropriateness on a defined scale (they used clinician ratings, "mean appropriateness score was 4.43 out of 5"). `WEAK-EVIDENCE` on generalizable "evidence-bound generation" benchmarks beyond the above (only 2 independent quantitative sources gathered).

## 5. Interactive / active diagnosis — the DEMAT prior-art landscape

**This is the most patent-sensitive section.** AegisMed's DEMAT (entropy-based active triage) claims novelty on *deciding what to ask next*. Published work in that lane, in order of strength:

1. **AMIE (Google; arXiv:2401.05654, v1 2024-01-11; fetched; published as Tu et al., *Nature*, 2025).** "AMIE (Articulate Medical Intelligence Explorer), a Large Language Model (LLM) based AI system optimized for diagnostic dialogue." Trained via "a novel self-play based simulated environment with automated feedback mechanisms for scaling learning across" (medical topics/contexts — sentence truncated in capture). Evaluation: "a randomized, double-blind crossover study of text-based" consultations; "The study included 149 case scenarios from clinical providers in Canada, the UK, and India, 20 PCPs for comparison with AMIE"; result: "AMIE demonstrated greater diagnostic accuracy and superior performance on 28 of 32 axes according to specialist physicians." AMIE's training objective explicitly includes information-seeking history-taking (details in full text; abstract quote unavailable). The published *Nature* version: "Towards conversational diagnostic artificial intelligence," nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08866-7 (page behind IDP redirect; fetch listed under UNKNOWN).
2. **AMIE DDx extension (McDuff et al., *Nature* 642:451–457, 2025-04-09; PMC12158753; fetched).** 302 (of "326 case texts") NEJM CPC cases, 20 clinicians: AMIE "provided the correct diagnosis in 177 (59%) of the DDx lists and in 89 (29%) of the lists it was at the top"; "The top-10 accuracy of AMIE (59.1%) was significantly higher than the top-10 accuracy for the unassisted clinicians (33.6%)"; clinician assistance lift: assisted "51.7%" vs unassisted "36.1%" (and search-assisted 44.4%). Also notable: "For n = 1, GPT-4 performs marginally better but not statistically significantly." **This is the published ceiling for top-1 on genuinely hard cases — ~29% — versus AegisMed's 98% on self-generated cases.**
3. **Q4Dx (Werthaim et al., *Scientific Reports* 16:6121, 2026-01-24; fetched).** "a benchmark for assessing goal-directed diagnostic questioning"; "we randomly select 100 disease–symptom pairings" instantiated at "multiple information levels (100%, 80%, and 50% symptom exposure)"; metrics include "Mean Questions to Correct Diagnosis (MQD)" with questioning capped at five. Results: "GPT-4.1 achieves a ZDA of 51% when provided with the complete set of symptoms (100% case), which declines to 44% and 37%" as symptom information is removed; "We report a Mean Questions to Correct Diagnosis (MQD) of 2.4." Crucially for the patent: "multi-step question-answering tasks in fields such as gaming and education use metrics like information gain or turn count, similar standards have not yet been widely adopted in medicine." **Both edges cut: (a) the field says information-gain questioning standards are NOT yet adopted in medicine (good for AegisMed); (b) a Jan-2026 benchmark already exists that measures exactly DEMAT's task (bad for "first-ever" claims).**
4. **Multimodal AMIE (arXiv:2505.04653)** — LLMs requesting medical tests (labs/imaging) during dialogue = value-of-information over test selection. Adjacent prior art.
5. **DIVA ("Decision-Informed Value-of-information Acquisition," arXiv, 2026)** — VoI-based information-seeking procedure for LLMs. Adjacent prior art (listed in arXiv new listings 2026; primary fetch not done). `WEAK-EVIDENCE`.
6. **Classical decision theory** (value-of-information in diagnosis predates LLMs by decades) — a patent claim on "entropy/information-gain question selection" *per se* will hit this; the claim must be tied to the concrete mechanism (deterministic rule-based extraction → entropy over the 15-condition graph → bounded 12-question bank → triage bands) rather than the abstract idea.

**Positioning conclusion for DEMAT/EN-SCAF:** the defensible novelty is the *combination*: (i) deterministic, auditable symptom-extraction layer (no LLM in the loop for extraction), (ii) closed-form entropy computation over an explicit, inspectable condition graph sized for edge deployment (no learned policy, no model call per question), (iii) triage-band (not diagnosis-ranking) output with red-flag override. Each element individually exists in the literature; the patent must claim the specific pipeline and its measurable properties (cost/latency/auditability), not "interactive diagnosis via information gain."

## 6. Hallucination and safety failure modes + mitigations

**Measured rates.**

- Asgari et al., "A framework to assess clinical safety and hallucination rates of LLMs for medical text summarisation," *npj Digital Medicine* (2025; nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01670-7; page redirects via IDP — numbers from search snippet of the primary page): "We observed a 1.47% hallucination rate and a 3.45% omission rate." medRxiv preprint (2024-09-12, doi:10.1101/2024.09.12.24313556) adds that workflow/prompt changes "reduced major (24 to 10) and minor omission (114 to 74) counts" — i.e., **omissions are the bigger, more reducible failure class**. Flag: primary page not fully fetched. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` pending full-text capture.
- Omar et al., "Multi-model assurance analysis showing large language model hallucinations," *Communications Medicine* 2025 (nature.com/articles/s43856-025-01021-3): hallucination rates "50–82% across models/prompting methods" (search snippet) — i.e., **ungrounded free-text medical generation is catastrophically unreliable; the 1.47% figure only holds inside a constrained, structured, verified pipeline.**
- Fabricated references: Miao et al. [19]: 31% of vanilla ChatGPT nephrology citations "completely fabricated" (fetched). This is the canonical number to cite for "never let the report agent cite from memory."

**Mitigations with evidence.**

- **Grounding/retrieval:** Almanac +18% and near-total citation validity vs 62% [19]. RAG "can eliminate hallucinations" is NOT supported — post-RAG hallucinations persist in audits (towardsai 2025 practice reports; consistent with Asgari's residual 1.47%/3.45%).
- **Structured output + rule-based fallback:** AegisMed's rule-based synthesis path mirrors what the clinical-safety framework literature recommends (constrained generation over retrieved evidence, human-readable provenance). No peer-reviewed head-to-head of "rule-based fallback vs LLM-only" in diagnosis reports was found. `UNKNOWN — how to find out: search "structured output" OR "constrained decoding" clinical report generation 2024-2026; NPJ digit med safety frameworks.`
- **Reasoning models:** a 2026 industry audit (presenc.ai, secondary) reports ChatGPT "major-error rate of 4.8% when using reasoning" vs higher without — directional only. `WEAK-EVIDENCE`.
- **Human oversight:** uniformly recommended (EUSEM quote in §2; Omar et al. JMIR 2025 recommendations in 05 §3).

## Implications for AegisMed

1. **Re-baseline the benchmark claim before release or CHIL submission.** "98% top-1 on 200 self-referential synthetic cases" is not citable science — AgentClinic [6] shows sequential formats can cut static-benchmark accuracy by >10x. Rebuild the eval (src/eval/) as a sequential protocol: patient-agent holds symptom info, AegisMed must ask; measure top-1, top-3, top-10, MQD-style questions-to-correct, and triage-band accuracy. Use Q4Dx-style symptom-exposure degradation (100%/80%/50%) [11].
2. **Report top-k, not just top-1.** On hard real cases the published SOTA top-1 is ~29% vs 59.1% top-10 [10]. A public diagnostic assistant that returns one condition at 98% is lying by construction. Emit a ranked differential (AegisMed: 3–5 conditions) in the report agent (src/agents/synthesis/agent.py, src/agents/rule_based_synthesis.py) and in the API contract.
3. **Position DEMAT against AMIE/Q4Dx in the patent NOW.** The provisional must not claim "interactive diagnosis via information gain" broadly [9,11]. Claim the specific mechanism: deterministic rule-based extraction (src/agents/nlp/agent.py) → closed-form entropy over an explicit 15-condition graph (src/core/demat.py) → bounded question bank → red-flag triage override — with auditability/latency/cost as the claimed advantages over learned policies (AMIE self-play) and LLM-per-turn questioning.
4. **Add an MDAgents-style complexity gate to the orchestrator (src/agents/orchestrator/agent.py).** Route easy presentations to single-agent/rule-based path; escalate to full multi-agent + LLM synthesis only when DEMAT entropy stays high or red flags appear. This is published, defensible, and cuts OpenRouter free-tier token cost and latency; cite the 4.2%→11.8% adaptive gains [4].
5. **Do not add multi-agent debate for its own sake.** Published evidence says debate does not reliably beat self-consistency and is hyperparameter-brittle [8]. If AegisMed keeps "agent agreement" as a signal, derive it from independent runs over *different evidence slices* (one agent per retrieved guideline chunk) rather than agent-to-agent argumentation.
6. **Bind every report claim to a retrieved passage (guideline-bound generation).** 31% of ungrounded medical citations are fabricated [19]. Require the synthesis agent (src/agents/synthesis/agent.py) to attach passage IDs from src/agents/rag/agent.py to every recommendation; drop any sentence without a citation; evaluate citation validity explicitly in the eval suite.
7. **Fix the no-negation extractor before anything else — it is a triage-safety bug, not a quality issue.** The under-triage literature [14,15,16] shows the dangerous failure is missing urgency. "No fever, no cough, no chest pain" extracting three positives directly inflates DEMAT entropy toward non-emergent handling. Negation handling in src/agents/nlp/agent.py should be gated as release-blocking.
8. **Add a deterministic red-flag override above DEMAT.** Given AI triage sensitivity 58.3% [14] and 51.6% emergency under-triage in the wild [15], hard-code high-risk presentations (chest pain + risk factors, stroke FAST, sepsis criteria, suicidality) that bypass entropy and force emergency-band output. Keep it rule-based and auditable — it is also a patent-friendly safety layer.
9. **Evaluate the rule-based fallback as a first-class arm, not a footnote.** Compare LLM-synthesis vs rule-based-synthesis vs hybrid on the sequential eval: report hallucination rate, omission rate (Asgari-style framework [20]) and triage safety per arm. This becomes a CHIL paper result and a release gate.
10. **Free-tier model selection: cite HealthBench + MedicalAgentsBench methodology, not MedQA.** Choose OpenRouter models by HealthBench-style rubric scoring on AegisMed's own 500-passage guideline corpus (conversation quality, safety axes), and test whether pairing a reasoning model with the agent scaffold beats scaffold-on-instruct models at equal token budget [1,7].
