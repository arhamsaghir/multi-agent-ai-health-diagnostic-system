# Clinical NLP State of the Art (2020–2026) for Consumer-Symptom Understanding

**Deep-research report — AegisMed public-release workstream, doc 03**
Compiled October 2026. Evidence rules: every load-bearing claim has ≥3 independent sources; weaker claims are tagged **WEAK-EVIDENCE** (1–2 sources) and gaps are tagged **UNKNOWN**. Verbatim numbers include source, publisher, and date.

---

## TL;DR

1. **Negation is a solved research problem that AegisMed has not implemented.** The NegEx→ConText lineage gets negation F-measures of ~0.93 on clinical reports [2][3]; BERT-class models (NegBERT) reach token-level scope F1 of 92.36–95.68 [4]; a 2023 head-to-head showed a lightweight ALBERT at F1 0.991 vs optimized NegEx 0.921 on real radiology reports [5]. The 2010 i2b2/VA challenge's best assertion classifier hit F 0.936 across six assertion categories including "absent" and "associated with someone other than the patient" [1]. AegisMed's pattern extractor implements **none** of this — "no fever, no cough, no chest pain" yields three asserted symptoms.
2. **The bundled d4data/biomedical-ner-all checkpoint is not a quality gate.** Its model card publishes **zero evaluation metrics**; it is a DistilBERT (66.4M params) fine-tuned in 30 minutes on one laptop GPU over MACCROBAT2018, a ~200-case-report corpus with 107 noisy entity classes [11][12]. It should not ship as the "advanced" NER path without local evaluation.
3. **Domain encoders dominate benchmarks**: PubMedBERT's from-scratch domain pretraining set BLURB SOTA [7]; BioBERT reports ~85 F1 on i2b2 2010 NER [6]; Bio_ClinicalBERT is the standard drop-in clinical encoder [8]; Spark NLP's Bi-LSTM-CNN-Char proved production-grade NER at 81–94 F1 without BERT [10]. MedGemma (2025) and BioMistral-7B are credible LLM-grade alternatives but are generators, not extractors [18][20].
4. **Entity linking to SNOMED/UMLS is a mature tooling problem**: scispaCy's EntityLinker (UMLS ~3M concepts, HPO 16k) [17], QuickUMLS (F1 ~0.72–0.74, up to 135× faster than MetaMap; MIT license but requires an NLM UMLS license) [15][16], and MedCAT (F1 0.448–0.738 on open datasets, F1 > 0.94 hospital-transfer) [13][14]. MedCAT is now **archived** (July 2025) and relicensed (Elastic License 2.0) — a dependency risk [14].
5. **Lay-language normalization is the weakest link in the field, not just in AegisMed**: on COMETA (20k Reddit mentions → SNOMED CT), even the best of 20 linkers left "a significant performance gap to fill" [12]; the consumer-health-vocabulary literature reports ~48% of lay terms still jargon-like [26]. AegisMed's 60-entry canonical map is small but defensible *only* as a curated CHV layer — and it currently **converts negated phrases ("no energy") into asserted symptoms ("fatigue")** (verified in `src/data/preprocessing/text_preprocessor.py`).
6. **Multilingual/Hinglish medical NLP is genuinely low-resource**: no strong Hindi medical NER corpus exists (**UNKNOWN** for production-grade quality); word-level code-mixed LID is hard for langdetect-class tools [25]; a 2024 study of 24 LLMs on real Indian-patient data found "instruction tuned Indic models do not always perform well on Indic language queries" and "code-mixed ... queries ... pose challenges" [22].
7. **Small models beat LLMs for extraction on cost/latency/accuracy**: ChatGPT in 2023 had NER precision 0.66–0.86 but **recall 0.40–0.52**, "significantly lag[ging] ... current state-of-the-art models" [21]; DistilBERT-class encoders run in ~5–30 ms on CPU with ONNX/INT8 **WEAK-EVIDENCE** [9][30], while GPT-4o-mini costs $0.15/$0.60 per 1M tokens [28]. Fine-tuned small negation models outperform zero-shot LLMs on clinical reports [5][21][27].

**Bottom line for AegisMed:** the highest-value public-release fix is not "add a better NER model" — it is a **negation/experiencer/temporality layer + entity linking + a golden evaluation harness** on top of the existing deterministic extractor.

## 1. Negation and uncertainty detection

### 1.1 The NegEx → ConText lineage (the rule-based gold standard)

NegEx (Chapman et al., 2001) is a regular-expression trigger-phrase algorithm; the original evaluation on 1,000 discharge-summary sentences (1,235 findings) reported **specificity 94.5%, positive predictive value 84.5%, sensitivity 77.8%**, against a naive "no/denies" baseline (specificity 85.3%, PPV 68.4%, sensitivity 88.3%) [2]. ConText (Harkema et al., 2009) extends NegEx to three contextual properties — negation, experiencer (patient vs other), and temporality (historical/hypothetical) — evaluated on 240 reports across six types (radiology, ED, surgical pathology, echo, operative, discharge). Test-set F-measures: **negation 0.93 (all reports; per-type range 0.75–0.95), historical 0.76, hypothetical 0.86, experiencer 1.0** — with the crucial caveat that "Conditions experienced by someone other than the patient are very rarely found in our report set" (only 5 experiencer cases) [3]. The paper explicitly warns that "it is unlikely that the performance for Historical can be increased to levels comparable to Negation" [3].

Maintained Python implementations: `negspacy` (spaCy pipeline component, `jenojp/negspacy`) and `pyConTextNLP` (Chapman's own Python ConText) [33]. A 2017 comparison of NegEx / PyConTextNLP / SynNeg for scope delimitation exists [33]; spaCy's built-in parser is generally considered insufficient for clinical negation, so dedicated components are preferred [33] (WEAK-EVIDENCE on community consensus).

### 1.2 The benchmark: 2010 i2b2/VA (still the reference numbers)

The 2010 i2b2/VA challenge corpus is 394 training + 477 test de-identified reports from three institutions [1]. Best results across 22/21/16 participating systems [1]:

| Task | Best F-measure |
|---|---|
| Concept extraction | **0.852** (inexact match 0.924) |
| **Assertion classification** | **0.936** |
| Relation classification | **0.737** |

Assertion categories, verbatim: concepts classified "as being present, absent, or possible in the patient, conditionally present in the patient under certain circumstances, hypothetically present in the patient at some future point, and mentioned in the patient report but associated with someone other than the patient" [1]. This six-way assertion scheme — including a **family-history class** — remains the standard taxonomy that any production symptom interpreter should implement (see §6).

### 1.3 Transformer-based negation (NegBERT and after)

NegBERT (Qian et al., LREC 2020) applies BERT transfer learning to negation cue + scope resolution, achieving **token-level scope F1: 92.36 (Sherlock), 95.68 (BioScope abstracts), 91.24 (BioScope full papers), 90.95 (SFU review)** — "outperforming the previous state-of-the-art systems by a significant margin" [4]. NegBERT was additionally trained on the i2b2 2010 assertion corpus; its out-of-domain numbers are markedly weaker — on the Weng et al. 2023 radiology corpus, unoptimized NegBERT scored F1 0.393, and even retrained reached 0.981 only with in-domain fine-tuning [5].

### 1.4 The 2023 head-to-head that matters most for AegisMed

Weng et al. (JMIR Medical Informatics, 2023;11:e46348) retrained and compared rule-based vs transformer systems on 6,000 real radiology reports [5]:

| System (optimized) | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ALBERT** (lightweight transformer) | 0.991 | 0.992 | **0.991** |
| NegBERT (retrained) | 0.992 | 0.970 | 0.981 |
| NegEx | 0.886 | 0.958 | 0.921 |
| NegBio | 0.860 | 0.794 | 0.826 |

Key verbatim conclusions: "Lightweight transformer models, such as ALBERT, can outperform NegEx, NegBio, and NegBERT on more complex and diverse real-world radiology reports"; "BERT is more suitable than NegEx and NegBio for tasks involving multisentence context analysis"; but "fine-tuning based on the specific domain corpus and task is still essential regardless of the model used" [5]. The paper deliberately does **not** claim negation is solved — echoing Wu et al.'s "Negation's not solved" generalizability critique [5].

**Implication strength:** strong (3+ independent sources: [1][2][3][4][5]). A deterministic extractor with zero negation handling is roughly at the "baseline 0.68 PPV" tier of 2001, not the modern tier.

## 2. Symptom / entity extraction: encoder checkpoints and LLMs

### 2.1 The dominant biomedical/clinical encoders

- **BioBERT** (Lee et al., *Bioinformatics* 2020; arXiv:1901.08746): continual pretraining of BERT on PubMed+PMC. Reported NER F1 ≈ **92.02 (BC5CDR-chem), 86.85 (BC5CDR-disease), 89.13 (NCBI-disease), 85.16 (2010 i2b2/VA)** [6] — figures cross-referenced in two independent search summaries and the BLURB leaderboard; treat the exact decimals as **WEAK-EVIDENCE** until the paper table is re-verified (fetch of the abstract page does not carry the tables).
- **PubMedBERT** (Gu et al., *ACM Trans. Comput. Healthcare* 2021; arXiv:2007.15779): pretrained **from scratch** on PubMed with a domain vocabulary; "pretraining language models from scratch" in domains "with abundant unlabeled text" achieved "new state-of-the-art results across the board" on the BLURB benchmark (leaderboard: aka.ms/BLURB), and specifically argued that domain-specific pretraining beats continual pretraining of general BERT (the BioBERT/ClinicalBERT recipe) [7]. Microsoft maintains BLURB as the reference biomedical NLU benchmark [7].
- **Bio_ClinicalBERT** (Alsentzer et al., ClinicalNLP@NAACL 2019; arXiv:1904.03323): BERT-base continued on MIMIC-III; domain-specific models "improved results on three common clinical NLP tasks" but were "not as performant on two clinical de-identification tasks" [8]. HuggingFace IDs: `emilyalsentzer/Bio_ClinicalBERT`, `emilyalsentzer/Bio_Discharge_Summary_BERT` [8] (IDs widely documented; card-level F1 tables not re-verified — **UNKNOWN** for exact i2b2 2010 F1 from the card).
- **SciBERT** (Beltagy et al. 2019) and **BioLinkBERT** (Yasunaga et al. 2022): competitive scientific-domain encoders; BioLinkBERT trained on 40M linked PubMed abstracts. **UNKNOWN** — no AegisMed-relevant symptom-extraction benchmark numbers were verified for these two in this pass.
- **Spark NLP biomedical NER** (Kocaman et al., 2020; arXiv:2011.06315): a Bi-LSTM-CNN-Char model "without using heavy contextual embeddings like BERT" reached **BC4CHEMD 93.72% (+4.1), Species800 80.91% (+4.6), JNLPBA 81.29% (+5.2)** — SOTA on seven public biomedical benchmarks inside "a production-grade code base ... can scale up for training and inference in any Spark cluster" [10]. This is the existence proof that a CPU-friendly non-BERT extractor can be SOTA-grade.
- **GatorTron** (Yang et al., 2022; arXiv:2203.03540): scales clinical LMs to **8.9B parameters**, pretrained on **90B words** including 8.9B words of UF Health EHR text; ~**9.6% average improvement over five clinical NLP tasks** including NER [19]. Access is gated behind a UF Health data-use agreement — a real procurement constraint for a student project.
- **Clinical-T5** (Agrawal et al. 2022): T5-family clinical encoder-decoder; **UNKNOWN** — no benchmark figures verified in this pass.

### 2.2 d4data/biomedical-ner-all: what the model card actually says

Verified from the HuggingFace card [11]:

- Base: **distilbert-base-uncased**, 66.4M parameters, F32.
- Trained on **MACCROBAT2018** to "recognize the bio-medical entities (107 entities) from a given text corpus (case reports etc.)".
- **Reported metrics: none.** No F1, accuracy, or eval split is published on the card. Training stats on the card: "Training time: 30.16527 minutes", "1 x GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU".
- License: Apache-2.0. Limitations: none stated on the card.

MACCROBAT2018 is a ~**200 clinical case report** corpus (modified from MACCROBAT 2014) [12], and is studied in the literature specifically as a **small-sample** NER problem with annotation inconsistency and class imbalance across its 107 labels [12] (WEAK-EVIDENCE on the exact annotation-quality claims; the 200-report size is corroborated by two sources). Practical consequence: this checkpoint generalizes to free-text consumer symptom language at an **unmeasured** quality level, behind an env flag that is off by default. That is the correct default; the gap is that no evaluation harness exists to measure it before enabling (see Implications §8).

### 2.3 Modern LLM options (2024–2026)

- **BioMistral-7B** (Labrak et al., 2024; arXiv:2402.10373; HF `BioMistral/BioMistral-7B`): Mistral 7B continued-pretrained on PubMed Central; evaluated on 10 English medical QA benchmarks plus a 7-language multilingual extension — "the first large-scale multilingual evaluation of LLMs in the medical domain"; outperforms existing open medical models of its size, trails GPT-4-class models; quantized/merged lightweight variants released [18]. Exact MedQA/PubMedQA decimals: **UNKNOWN** (abstract carries no numbers).
- **MedGemma** (Google, May 2025; HF `google/medgemma-4b-it` / `-27b-it`): Gemma 3 variants "trained for performance on medical text and image comprehension". Verified card numbers for the 4B instruction-tuned model: **MedQA (4-op) 64.4, MedMCQA 55.7, PubMedQA 73.4, MIMIC-CXR macro F1 88.9** (vs base Gemma 3 4B: 50.7/45.4/68.4/81.2) [20]. The 27B text model is reported at 87.7% MedQA [20] (WEAK-EVIDENCE, secondary summary). Licensing: "Health AI Developer Foundations" terms, gated on HF. The card's own disclaimer is a template for AegisMed's release language: outputs are "not intended to directly inform clinical diagnosis, patient management decisions, treatment recommendations, or any other direct clinical practice applications," and "All outputs from MedGemma should be considered preliminary and require independent verification" [20].
- **GPT-4o-class proprietary LLMs**: see §7 — strong at QA, weak at token-level extraction.

**Which checkpoint "dominates"?** For consumer-symptom NER specifically there is no single dominant checkpoint; on standard benchmarks the pattern is: PubMedBERT-class domain-from-scratch encoders ≥ BioBERT/ClinicalBERT ≥ general BERT [7][8], with Spark NLP's non-BERT Bi-LSTM-CNN-Char competitive for production throughput [10].

## 3. Entity linking to terminologies (UMLS, SNOMED CT, ICD)

### 3.1 Tools and measured accuracy

| Tool | Linking accuracy (as published) | License / status |
|---|---|---|
| **QuickUMLS** (Soldaini & Goharian, MedIR@SIGIR 2016) | F1 **0.72** vs cTAKES 0.68 vs MetaMap 0.61; "up to 135× faster" [15]. Independent re-eval: **0.7409** (QuickUMLS) vs 0.7321 (MetaMap) on Australian clinical notes [16] | MIT; **requires an NLM UMLS license** (user installs MRCONSO.RRF/MRSTY.RRF) [15] |
| **scispaCy EntityLinker** (Neumann et al., BioNLP@ACL 2019) | MedMentions-based; exact F1 not on abstract [17]. Ships linkers for **UMLS (~3M concepts, levels 0,1,2,9), MeSH (~30k), RxNorm (~100k), GO (~67k), HPO (16k)**; char-3gram ANN search, ~1 GB UMLS KB download on first use [17] | Apache-2.0 |
| **MedCAT** (Kraljevic et al., *Artif Intell Med* 2021) | "improved performance in extracting UMLS concepts from open datasets (F1: 0.448–0.738 vs 0.429–0.650)" vs prior systems; "strong transferability (F1 > 0.94) between hospitals, datasets, and concept types" [13]. Meta-annotation (negation/experiencer/temporality) trained on i2b2/VA 2010 at ~0.90 F1 **WEAK-EVIDENCE** (secondary summaries only) | Was Apache-2.0, **moved to Elastic License 2.0 in 2022**; repo **archived 28 Jul 2025**, successor `CogStack/cogstack-nlp` [14] |
| **MetaMap** (NLM) | F1 0.61 [15]; 0.7321 [16] — consistently the slowest/lowest of the three | NLM tool, aging |
| **cTAKES** (Apache) | F1 0.68 [15] | Apache-2.0 |

Verbatim from the MedCAT repo: it links to SNOMED-CT and UMLS, with entity extraction "trained on MedMentions" (~35K UMLS entities) and a **MetaCAT** component for context meta-annotations such as negation [14].

### 3.2 Terminology grounding

SNOMED CT is "a standardised clinical terminology consisting of >350,000 unique concepts" [14]; UMLS integrates ~4.4M concepts across 200+ vocabularies (Metathesaurus; scispaCy's linker exposes ~3M of them) [17]. For a symptom checker the pragmatic mapping targets are: **SNOMED CT clinical findings** (COMETA ground truth [12]), **HPO for phenotypes** (16k concepts, curated for symptoms) [17], and **ICD-10/ICD-11** for report coding (typically reached via SNOMED↔ICD maps; **UNKNOWN** — no verified open benchmark for lay-symptom→ICD linking was found).

### 3.3 The production symptom-checker pipeline shape

Publicly documented architectures are thin (proprietary), but the recurring shape across Ada Health and Babylon-heritage systems is: **free text → normalization to a curated concept vocabulary → structured follow-up questioning → Bayesian/knowledge-graph reasoning over an evidence-mapped condition set → triage** [32][34]. Babylon explicitly built a "Medical Knowledge Graph" (Schulz et al., CEUR-WS Vol-2849) for evidence mapping, and its reasoner was a Bayesian network [34]. No public evidence that Ada/Babyon use UMLS directly — **UNKNOWN/proprietary**; academic symptom-extraction pipelines standardize on UMLS/SNOMED via the tools above [15][17][13]. This means AegisMed's deterministic pattern layer over ~11 symptom categories [35] is *architecturally legitimate* — what is missing is the normalization and context layers around it.

## 4. Symptom normalization: lay language → clinical terms

- **COMETA** (Basaldella et al., EMNLP 2020; arXiv:2010.03295) is the reference benchmark: "20k English biomedical entity mentions from Reddit expert-annotated with links to SNOMED CT" [12]. Verbatim verdict after benchmarking 20 linkers (string- to neural-based): "no golden bullet exists and even the best mainstream techniques still have a significant performance gap to fill, while the best solution relies on combining different views of data" [12]. Exact best-model accuracy: **UNKNOWN** from the abstract (tables not re-verified).
- **Consumer Health Vocabulary (OAC CHV)** (Zeng & Tse 2006; maintained by GWU): a curated mapping of consumer expressions to UMLS concepts; "focuses on expressions and concepts that are employed by health-related communications from or to consumers" [26]. A 2020 enrichment study found CHV terms map to ~**56,000 professional UMLS concepts** and ~**48% of laymen's terms were still jargon-like** [26] (WEAK-EVIDENCE — single arXiv source). Keselman et al. (2008) documented consumer concepts that do not map to UMLS at all [26].
- **LLM-based normalization (2023–2026)**: README (arXiv:2312.15561) benchmarks LLMs on medical-jargon↔lay-language conversion and notes CHV dictionary limitations [26]; Guo et al. (JBI/PMC10874606, 2024) show RAG over CHV improves LLM lay-language generation [26]. Direction of travel: LLMs outperform dictionary lookup for lay↔clinical translation, but hallucination risk means outputs need verification, and CHV itself "hasn't been updated since ~2011" [26] (WEAK-EVIDENCE on the update-date claim).
- **ADE corpora** (ADE, ADE-Corpus-V2, PSB 2019 ADE) target adverse-drug-event extraction rather than lay symptom normalization — adjacent but **not** the primary resource for this problem (**UNKNOWN** for a direct lay-symptom ADE benchmark in this pass).

**Assessment of AegisMed's `SYMPTOM_CANONICAL`** (60 phrase→canonical entries in `src/data/preprocessing/text_preprocessor.py`) [35]: it is a miniature OAC CHV. Its size (dozens vs tens of thousands of entries) is a coverage gap, but its bigger defect is ordering: it applies blind `str.replace` **before** negation handling, so "no energy" → "fatigue" and "no appetite" → "decreased appetite" are injected into the text as asserted symptoms [35]. That inverts the polarity of negated lay phrases — exactly the class of error COMETA/CHV work exists to fix.

## 5. Multilingual symptom NLP (Hindi/Hinglish and Indic languages)

- **Resource scarcity is the headline.** Indian languages remain low-resource for NLP (Hindi NLP resource surveys document this directly) [25]. For code-mixed Hinglish, foundational NER work exists (Singh et al., W18-2405, 2018) and COMI-LINGUA (arXiv:2503.21670) is described as the largest expert-annotated Hindi-English code-mixed dataset covering NER/POS/MT [25]. **No production-grade Hindi/Hinglish *medical* NER corpus was found — UNKNOWN.** Naamii (NepAl Applied Mathematics and Informatics Institute for research) is an active low-resource-language NLP research group, but **no Naamii-organized medical-entity shared task could be verified — UNKNOWN** (their project index lists Nepali LMs and COVID tweet classification, not medical NER) [25].
- **Cross-lingual transfer numbers**: "Multilingual Clinical NER: Translation or Cross-lingual Transfer?" (arXiv:2306.04384) tested English→French/German clinical NER without target data and concluded "translation-based methods can achieve similar performance to CLT but require more care in their design" and — notably for AegisMed — "monolingual clinical language models ... do not guarantee better results than large general-purpose multilingual models" [24]. XLM-R is the standard backbone; zero-shot drops of ~10–30 F1 points vs in-language fine-tuning are typical in the literature **WEAK-EVIDENCE** (summary-level; no single table verified).
- **Hindi medical QA reality check** — HEALTH-PARIKSHA (arXiv:2410.13671, v3 Dec 2025): "an extensive assessment of 24 LLMs on real world data collected from Indian patients" found "instruction tuned Indic models do not always perform well on Indic language queries", that "code-mixed and culturally relevant queries in our dataset pose challenges to evaluated models", and factual correctness is "generally lower" for Indic-language responses than English [22]. Airavata (arXiv:2401.15006, AI4Bharat, Jan 2024) is a 7B Hindi instruction-tuned LLM (fine-tuned from OpenHathi) and one of the stronger Indic options [23].
- **Language detection pitfalls in code-switched text**: langdetect-class tools assume single-language documents and native scripts; **romanized Hinglish defeats them** because both languages share Latin-script strings with spelling variance; word-level character-n-gram/embedding classifiers reach ~88–97% accuracy in the literature [25] (WEAK-EVIDENCE on the specific accuracy range). This directly affects `src/utils/language_detector.py`, which uses `langdetect.detect()` on the whole message and returns `"en"` on failure [35].

## 6. Context attribution: patient vs family history vs medication vs hypothetical

- The **2010 i2b2/VA assertion taxonomy** is the canonical formulation: present / absent / possible / conditional / hypothetical / "associated with someone other than the patient" [1]. Best system F 0.936 overall [1].
- **ConText** operationalizes experiencer+temporality, but its own evaluation shows how thin the data is: experiencer F 1.0 was computed from **5 cases** ("very rarely found in our report set") and historical F 0.76 — the authors' conclusion is that historical status "requires knowledge above and beyond the surface clues" [3]. Practical read: rule-based experiencer detection works when families are mentioned explicitly ("my mother has diabetes") but is unproven beyond that.
- **MedCAT's MetaCAT** ships exactly this meta-annotation layer (negation / experiencer / temporality classifiers attached to linked concepts) trained on i2b2/VA 2010 [13][14] — the same shape AegisMed needs between its extractor and its triage agent.
- **2012 i2b2 temporal challenge** (Sun, Rumshisky, Uzuner, JAMIA 2013;20:806–813): corpus of 310 discharge summaries (190 train/120 test) annotated for events, timex, and TLINKs across five report modules including **FAMILY_HISTORY** [29]. Best TLINK F-measure **0.7111**; event/timex extraction ~0.85–0.90 **WEAK-EVIDENCE** on the exact event/timex decimals (two secondary summaries; overview-paper tables not directly fetched). The durable finding: **temporal/contextual relations stay ~15–20 F1 points below entity extraction** — context is the hard part everywhere.
- **Medication-effect attribution** (symptom caused by a drug rather than disease): handled in the ADE-corpus literature and pharmacovigilance NLP; **UNKNOWN** — no verified benchmark for lay descriptions ("since I started metformin I feel nauseous") was found in this pass.

## 7. Small models vs LLMs for on-CPU extraction

**Accuracy.** For token-level extraction, fine-tuned small models beat zero-shot LLMs. John Snow Labs' evaluation (April 2023): ChatGPT medical NER "Precision ranged between 0.66 to 0.86, and recall was particularly problematic at between 0.40 and 0.52", with these models "significantly lag[ging] in accuracy compared to current state-of-the-art models" (their fine-tuned healthcare NER hit 93% on de-identification vs ChatGPT's 60%) [21]. A 2025 Nature Digital Medicine benchmark of six medical LLMs found "moderate overall performance (average total score 57.2%)" [27] (WEAK-EVIDENCE on exact decimals). Counterweight: in-domain-fine-tuned ALBERT reached F1 0.991 on radiology negation [5] — a small model.

**Latency (CPU).** DistilBERT: "40% smaller", "60% faster", retains "97% of language understanding capabilities" [9] (verified from the paper's abstract page). ONNX Runtime + INT8 quantization on BERT-class models lands at roughly **5–30 ms/sample on CPU** (a published HF-Optimum benchmark reports ~9.75 ms/inference; ORT gives ~1.5–2× over PyTorch; dynamic INT8 adds ~1.8×) [30] — **all WEAK-EVIDENCE** (engineering blog benchmarks, not peer-reviewed; no peer-reviewed $/1k-request figure found — UNKNOWN). The d4data checkpoint already runs on this profile (66M params) [11]; 7–9B models (BioMistral, GatorTron, MedGemma-27B) are not CPU-realistic for interactive latency [18][19][20].

**Cost.** GPT-4o-mini: **$0.15 / $0.60 per 1M input/output tokens** (July 2024 launch pricing, DeepLearning.AI) vs GPT-4o at $2.50 / $10.00 [28]. AegisMed's extraction volume (a few hundred tokens per message) makes even LLM normalization cheap per call — but the recall argument [21] says LLMs should not own extraction.

## Implications for AegisMed

Concrete changes to this repository, in priority order for public release:

1. **Add a NegEx/ConText-style assertion layer inside `src/agents/nlp/agent.py`** (highest value, zero model cost). Before `_extract_symptoms` runs, scan for pre-triggers (`no|not|denies|without|never|resolved|rule out|ruled out`) and post-triggers (`unlikely|negative for|free of`) within a bounded window (e.g., 4 tokens before the match to the next conjunction/"but"), plus a pseudo-negation filter ("not only", "no allergies to X but..."). Emit each symptom as `{symptom, category, assertion: present|absent|possible, experiencer: patient|other, temporality: current|historical|hypothetical}` instead of today's bare `{symptom, category}`. Only `assertion == "present"` symptoms should flow to the entropy-based triage agent; absent symptoms are still valuable (pertinent negatives belong in the LLM report as positive evidence). The 2010 i2b2/VA six-way assertion taxonomy [1] is the spec; NegEx's measured trade-off (PPV 84.5% at sensitivity 77.8% [2]) is the expected band for a rules-only implementation.

2. **Fix the polarity bug in `src/data/preprocessing/text_preprocessor.py`.** (a) `normalize_symptoms()` must not run blind `str.replace` for entries whose lay phrase contains a negator — "no appetite"→"decreased appetite" and "no energy"→"fatigue" currently manufacture asserted symptoms from negations [35]. Extract the canonical term plus the assertion flag instead. (b) Remove `"without"` from `STOP_WORDS` — it is a negation trigger, not noise. (c) Make `normalize_symptoms` boundary-aware (`re.sub(rf"\b{re.escape(variant)}\b")`) since substring replaces can corrupt longer phrases. Add regression tests for "no fever, no cough, no chest pain", "denies chest pain", "no appetite but eating fine".

3. **Add experiencer/hypothetical patterns before triage.** Family-history and hypothetical markers ("my mother had", "family history of", "what if", "if I get", "worried that I might", "friend of mine") should tag the entity `experiencer: other` or `temporality: hypothetical` and be excluded from (or down-weighted in) the entropy triage over the 15 conditions. ConText's own data shows these cues are rare and mostly explicit [3], so patterns suffice for v1 — this matches the i2b2 "associated with someone other than the patient" class [1] and MedCAT's MetaCAT design [13][14].

4. **Rethink `config/settings.py` `enable_biobert_ner` / `biobert_model_id`.** `d4data/biomedical-ner-all` is a DistilBERT fine-tuned for 30 minutes on ~200 case reports with 107 noisy labels and **no published metrics** [11][12] — it cannot be presented as the "advanced" path in a public release without local numbers. Options: (a) keep it flag-gated but add `ner_min_confidence` and a label whitelist (drop the 107-class noise down to symptom/sign/disease/medication classes it actually detects well); (b) add an alternative `biobert_model_id` default such as `emilyalsentzer/Bio_ClinicalBERT` fine-tuned on i2b2-style symptom data, or scispaCy's `en_ner_bc5cdr_md` [17]; (c) ship the deterministic extractor as the documented default and require an evaluation report before the NER path is advertised. Also add settings for the new assertion layer (`enable_negation_detection: bool = True`) and entity linking (`entity_linker: "none"|"scispacy"|"quickumls"`).

5. **Add an entity-linking module** (`src/data/preprocessing/` or new `src/nlp/linking.py`) mapping extracted symptoms to canonical codes for the synthesized report and FHIR output. Recommended stack for a CPU-only, Apache-2.0-safe project: **scispaCy EntityLinker with the HPO linker (16k phenotype concepts) or UMLS linker** [17] — but note QuickUMLS (MIT) requires the user to obtain an NLM UMLS license [15], and MedCAT is now Elastic-licensed and archived (successor: cogstack-nlp) [14], so avoid a hard dependency on it. Keep `SYMPTOM_CANONICAL` as the curated lay-language front layer (a miniature OAC CHV [26]) — COMETA shows pure string/linking systems fail on layman terms, and hybrid "different views of data" is the best strategy [12]. Target SNOMED CT/HPO codes in the report; ICD mapping via SNOMED maps (**UNKNOWN** for lay-level ICD quality — disclose as approximation).

6. **Build the golden evaluation harness in `tests/unit/test_nlp_agent.py`** before any public claim of accuracy. Create a fixture golden set (≥100 utterances) covering: asserted symptoms, negated ("no fever"), pertinent negatives, lay paraphrases ("tummy ache", "head is pounding"), family history, hypotheticals, durations, severities, and Hinglish romanizations. Measure symptom-extraction precision/recall/F1 (strict + category-level) and assertion-classification accuracy, and assert thresholds in CI (e.g., negated-symptom false-positive count == 0 for the canonical failing case). Run the same harness over `enable_biobert_ner=true` to produce the missing model card numbers [11]. This is the single artifact that converts the system from "unmeasured" to "publicly defensible".

7. **Keep LLMs out of extraction; use them for normalization assist and synthesis only.** Published ChatGPT NER recall was 0.40–0.52 vs fine-tuned small models [21], and medical-LLM benchmark averages hover ~57% [27]. Concretely: do not replace `_extract_symptoms` with an LLM call; optionally add an LLM fallback that proposes *new lay phrases* for `SYMPTOM_CANONICAL` (assisted CHV growth [26]) offline, not at request time. In `src/agents/rule_based_synthesis.py`/report synthesis, render pertinent negatives explicitly ("you reported no fever, no cough — this lowers...") — this is both clinically honest and free.

8. **Multilingual path hardening.** `src/utils/language_detector.py` uses whole-message `langdetect`, which fails on romanized Hinglish [25]. Add a token-level heuristic (Devanagari ratio + common Hinglish lexicon) and treat code-mixed input as a first-class case; given HEALTH-PARIKSHA's finding that Indic models underperform and code-mixed queries challenge even frontier LLMs [22], set the Hindi/Hinglish experience to translation-then-extract with an explicit quality disclaimer rather than advertising native multilingual triage. Document Hindi medical NER as unavailable (**UNKNOWN** per §5) in release notes.

9. **Latency/cost guardrails for the NER path.** The pattern extractor is ~microseconds; the NER path should stay DistilBERT-class on CPU. Apply ONNX Runtime export + dynamic INT8 quantization for a ~2–4× speedup (reported ~10–30 ms/sample) [9][30] — note the WEAK-EVIDENCE tag and measure locally in the harness. Set a hard timeout on NER inference with fallback to patterns (the code already falls back on load failure [35]; extend it to latency). Do not route extraction through BioMistral/MedGemma/GatorTron [18][19][20] — 7–9B models are not CPU-interactive, and MedGemma/GatorTron carry gated licenses/terms.

10. **Public-release disclaimer language.** Mirror the MedGemma card's standard in `docs/` and the report footer: outputs are "preliminary and require independent verification" and "not intended to directly inform clinical diagnosis ... or any other direct clinical practice applications" [20]. Pair each generated differential with the evidence provenance (which symptoms were asserted, which were negated, which were family history) — the assertion layer from item 1 is what makes that provenance truthful.
