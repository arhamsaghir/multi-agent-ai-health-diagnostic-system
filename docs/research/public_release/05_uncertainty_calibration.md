# 05 — Uncertainty, Calibration & Conformal Methods in Medical AI

**Scope:** S5 of the public-release research program. Calibration fundamentals in clinical prediction; conformal prediction for diagnosis/triage (APS/RAPS/CQR, coverage numbers, risk control); uncertainty in LLM medical output (verbalized, token-level, sampling-based, conformal); Bayesian/evidential methods; what FDA/CE guidance expects about uncertainty quantification.
**Compiled:** 2026-10-27. **Evidence protocol:** README.md rules. Verbatim quotes captured from arXiv (API/full pages), PMC, FDA pages and journal pages where fetchable; snippets are flagged.

---

## TL;DR

1. **AegisMed's ECE of 0.245 is clinically disqualifying as a "confidence."** An ECE of 0.245 means the stated confidence deviates from realized accuracy by ~24.5 percentage points on average — in the published clinical literature, miscalibration directly destroys net benefit even when discrimination is fine, and the regulatory direction (FDA/Health Canada/MHRA transparency principles) explicitly expects "confidence intervals associated with the provided outputs" [1,11,12].
2. **LLMs' self-reported confidence is nearly useless as a probability.** Across 12 LLMs and 1,965 clinical MCQs, mean confidence was 76.1% when correct vs 74.4% when wrong — a 0.6–5.4 pp separation — and confidence-accuracy correlation was *inverse* (r=−0.40, P=.001) [6]. Verbalized confidence is systematically overconfident [5]. **Do not let any LLM emit AegisMed's user-facing confidence number.**
3. **Conformal prediction is the standard fix, and it is now medical-mainstream.** APS/RAPS give prediction sets containing the true label "with a user-specified probability, such as 90%" [8]; risk-controlling prediction sets (RCPS) add finite-sample control of user-chosen losses like FNR [9]; conformal methods have been applied to medical MCQA with guarantees met while "reducing average prediction set size" [10], and to biomedical imaging [13]. A 90%-target medical application reported 90.4% empirical coverage [14].
4. **Conformal guarantees break under exactly AegisMed's architecture choice.** "Conformity Breaks Conformal Prediction" (2026): under unanimous-but-wrong multi-agent peers, coverage "falls from a calibrated 90% to 74%" at α=0.10, and targeted attacks on low-confidence items drop subgroup coverage "from 87% to 47%" [15]. AegisMed's agreement-blended confidence must be conformalized *before* agent blending, or per-agent, not after.
5. **Evidential deep learning (Dirichlet) offers single-pass uncertainty but is mostly validated on imaging** — including against entropy/MC-dropout/deep-ensemble baselines [17,18] — and is a heavier lift than split-conformal for AegisMed's tabular GradientBoosting stack. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` for EDL on symptom-tabular diagnosis specifically.
6. **Regulatory floor:** FDA's (re-issued, final January 2026) CDS guidance is the gate for whether AegisMed is device or non-device CDS [11]; the joint transparency principles name the information users must get, including performance details and confidence intervals [12]; GMLP principles (Oct 2021; IMDRF final document Jan 2025) set the development bar [16]. EU MDR/CE specifics: `UNKNOWN — how to find out` (defer to 07_regulatory_pathways.md).

## 1. Calibration fundamentals in clinical prediction

**Why uncalibrated confidence harms clinical use.**

- Myers et al., "Identifying unreliable predictions in clinical risk models," *npj Digital Medicine* 3:8 (2020-01-23; PMC6978376; fetched). Even a model that is "well calibrated over the entire dataset as assessed using a Hosmer–Lemeshow test" (their GRACE example) hid subgroup failures: "Calibration curves demonstrate that patients within the upper 50% of unreliability values tend to overestimate a patient's risk of death." Their safety argument is verbatim: "poor performance for clinical models can have deleterious consequences for patients," and models that cannot discriminate within a subgroup "should not be used for patients who belong to that subgroup, as such results are potentially harmful." **Lesson for AegisMed: dataset-level ECE is necessary but not sufficient; subgroup (per-condition, per-presentation) calibration is the real requirement.**
- la Roi-Teeuw et al. (external validation of clinical prediction models; PDF hosted at hbiostat.org, snippet-captured): miscalibration "always reduced net benefit" on external validation, and external validation "frequently reveals worse performance" than reported. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (snippet; full PDF unfetchable by tool). Corroborated directionally by Verhaeghe et al., *Journal of Biomedical Informatics* 2023 (ECE-measured calibration of atrial-fibrillation prediction models; snippet) and by Dong et al. 2024 (PMC11217160), which monitors deployed cardiac-surgery models with **1 − ECE**, discrimination, decision-curve net benefit and adjusted Brier score — i.e., ECE/Brier are the operational drift metrics in deployed clinical ML. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (snippet).
- Canonical framing (title-level citation; page blocked): Van Calster B, et al. "Calibration: the Achilles heel of predictive analytics." *BMC Medicine* 17:230 (2019) — good discrimination (AUC) does not imply calibration; decisions calibrated on predicted probabilities are what determine net benefit. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (title/citation verified only).

**Metrics and targets.**

- ECE (expected calibration error): weighted absolute gap between confidence bin means and accuracy; Brier score: mean squared probability error. Both are standard in the deployed-model monitoring literature above [3].
- **AegisMed's ECE = 0.245** (in-repo measured). There is no universally published "acceptable ECE" for clinical triage. `UNKNOWN — how to find out: survey calibration reporting in npj Digital Medicine/JAMIA triage papers 2023-2026 for the empirical distribution of ECE in published clinical models; typical deep-learning baselines in Guo et al. (NeurIPS 2017) report ECE ≈ 0.05–0.15 pre-calibration, which suggests 0.245 is poor, but treat that as a heuristic, not a standard.`
- Practical reading for AegisMed: with 15 conditions, ECE 0.245 ≈ on average the tool's stated confidence should be read as ~24–25 points lower. If the UI shows "87% confident," the honest calibrated statement is "roughly 60–65% of the time this confidence bucket is right — and only on distribution."

## 2. Conformal prediction for diagnosis / triage

**Methods (verified primary sources).**

- **APS/RAPS** — Angelopoulos AN, Bates S, Malik J, Jordan MI. "Uncertainty Sets for Image Classifiers using Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2009.14193, v1 2020-09-29; ICLR 2021 Spotlight; fetched via arXiv API). Verbatim: "We present an algorithm that modifies any classifier to output a predictive set containing the true label with a user-specified probability, such as 90%." RAPS = "modifies an existing conformal prediction algorithm to give more stable predictive sets by regularizing the small scores of unlikely classes after Platt scaling." **APS/RAPS are the drop-in choice for AegisMed's 15-way classifier.**
- **RCPS (risk-controlling prediction sets)** — Bates S, Angelopoulos A, Lei L, Malik J, Jordan MI. "Distribution-Free, Risk-Controlling Prediction Sets." arXiv:2101.02703 (v1 2021-01-07; JACM 68(6), 2021; fetched via arXiv API). Verbatim: builds set-valued predictions that "control the expected loss on future test points at a user-specified level," with "explicit finite-sample guarantees for any dataset" via a holdout set. Demonstrated on classification with costly mistakes, multi-label, hierarchical classification, segmentation, protein structure. **This is the method for controlling the false-negative rate of AegisMed's emergency triage band.**
- **CQR (conformalized quantile regression)** — Romano Y, Patterson E, Candès E, NeurIPS 2019 (arXiv:1905.03222): distribution-free interval coverage for regression — the analogue for AegisMed's continuous risk scores from GradientBoosting. Method citation only; page not fetched this pass. `WEAK-EVIDENCE`.
- **Conformal risk control** — generalizes RCPS to arbitrary monotone losses (Angelopoulos et al., arXiv:2208.02814, ICLR 2024). Method citation only; not fetched. `WEAK-EVIDENCE`.

**Medical applications with actual coverage numbers.**

- **Medical MCQA conformal (Ke Y, Lin H, Ruan Y, Tang J, Li L. arXiv:2503.05505, v1 2025-03-07, v2 2025-05-08; fetched via arXiv API).** "Conformal Prediction (CP) provides a statistically rigorous framework for marginal (average) coverage guarantees but has limited exploration in medical QA." Their enhanced framework ties the non-conformance score to "the frequency score of correct options and leveraging self-consistency"; evaluated "on MedMCQA, MedQA, and MMLU datasets using four off-the-shelf LLMs"; result: "the proposed method meets specified error rate guarantees while reducing average prediction set size with increased risk level." **Closest published analogue to conformalizing AegisMed's LLM diagnosis path.**
- **Black-box conformal MCQA (Yang G, Liu X. arXiv:2508.05544, v1 2025-08-07; fetched via arXiv API).** "frequency-based uncertainty quantification method under black-box settings, leveraging conformal prediction (CP) to ensure provable coverage guarantees"; "Experimental evaluations across six LLMs and four datasets (MedMCQA, MedQA, MMLU, MMLU-Pro) demonstrate that frequency-based PE outperforms logit-based PE in distinguishing between correct and incorrect predictions, as measured by AUROC." **Relevant because OpenRouter free-tier access is black-box (no logits) — frequency-based non-conformity is exactly what AegisMed can implement.**
- **Biomedical imaging (Davenport S. arXiv:2410.03406, v1 2024-10-04; fetched via arXiv API).** "Conformal confidence sets for biomedical image segmentation": sets "guaranteed to contain the true unknown segmented mask with desired probability" while controlling "the false coverage rate"; polyp segmentation; notes that learning score transformations on a separate dataset is "crucial for optimizing performance."
- **Deployed-style empirical coverage (medRxiv, Dec 2025, doi:10.64898/2025.12.14.25342221; snippet only, page 403):** "The conformal prediction (CP) framework provided an overall empirical coverage of 90.4%, exceeding the target threshold of 90%." `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (snippet).
- **Reviews:** Vazquez & Facelli, "Conformal Prediction in Clinical Medical Sciences," *Journal of Personalized Medicine* 12(2):244 (2022) — CP "can be used in clinical applications to provide important insight into the accuracy of individual predictions" (Duke scholars page snippet). Wang et al., "Conformal Prediction in Clinical Artificial Intelligence" (PubMed 41672654, 2026 review) — reliability/interpretability in pathology/diagnostics. `WEAK-EVIDENCE` (snippet-level).

**The failure mode that matters for AegisMed (fetched; arXiv:2609.04445, v1 2026-09-03).** "Conformity Breaks Conformal Prediction" (Hu Y, Su H): conformal calibration "certifies a model's solo scoring, 'but not how it scores them under peer pressure.'" In multi-agent LLM settings with unanimous-but-wrong peers, coverage "falls from a calibrated 90% to 74%" at α=0.10 on multiple-choice QA; "Targeted attacks on low-confidence items drop subgroup coverage from 87% to 47%"; and standard conformal fixes don't help "since the question distribution is unchanged." **AegisMed's confidence_scorer blends agent availability/agreement — i.e., it injects peer agreement into the score after any calibration. Per this paper, that blending is precisely what destroys the guarantee. Calibrate per-agent/per-evidence-slice scores, then aggregate with a method that preserves validity (e.g., calibrate the ensemble end-to-end on held-out data where agents can be jointly wrong), never trust post-blend confidence.**

**Practical numbers to design against.** Conformal calibration needs a held-out calibration set; ~1,000 examples is the commonly recommended minimum for stable quantiles (practice guidance repeated across tutorials; `WEAK-EVIDENCE`). At 95% target coverage on a 15-class problem, expect set sizes of ~1–4 for confident presentations and >6 for ambiguous ones; if sets are routinely huge, the underlying model is too weak for the guarantee to be useful — fix the model, then conformalize.

## 3. Uncertainty in LLM medical output

**Verbalized confidence is overconfident (multiple independent sources).**

- Xiong M, et al. "Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty? An Empirical Evaluation of Confidence Elicitation in LLMs." arXiv:2306.13063 (v1 2023-06-22; ICLR 2024; fetched via arXiv API). Verbatim: "1) LLMs, when verbalizing their confidence, tend to be overconfident, potentially imitating human patterns of expressing confidence. 2) As model capability scales up, both calibration and failure prediction performance improve. 3) Employing our proposed strategies, such as human-inspired prompts, consistency among multiple responses, and better aggregation strategies can help mitigate this overconfidence … 4) Comparisons with white-box methods indicate that while white-box methods perform better, the gap is narrow, e.g., 0.522 to 0.605 in AUROC." And critically: "all investigated methods struggle in challenging tasks, such as those requiring professional knowledge" — i.e., **medicine is in the hardest regime for LLM self-uncertainty.**
- Tian K, et al. "Just Ask for Calibration." arXiv:2305.14975 (v1 2023-05-24; EMNLP 2023; fetched via arXiv API). Verbatim: "verbalized confidences emitted as output tokens are typically better-calibrated than the model's conditional probabilities on the TriviaQA, SciQ, and TruthfulQA benchmarks, often reducing the expected calibration error by a relative 50%" — while noting RLHF-LMs' conditional probabilities "are very poorly calibrated." Definition quoted: "A trustworthy real-world prediction system should produce well-calibrated confidence scores … enabling deferral to an expert in cases of low-confidence predictions." **Best case: verbalized confidence is merely *relatively* better; it is still not a clinical probability.**
- Omar M, Agbareia R, Glicksberg BS, Nadkarni GN, Klang E. "Benchmarking the Confidence of Large Language Models in Answering Clinical Questions: Cross-Sectional Evaluation Study." *JMIR Medical Informatics* 2025 May 16;13:e66917 (PMC12101789; fetched). "This study evaluates the confidence levels of 12 LLMs across 5 medical specialties"; "We used 1965 multiple-choice questions." Numbers: "The mean confidence score for all 12 models was 76.1% when they were correct and 74.4% when they were incorrect"; "The mean difference in confidence between correct and incorrect responses was low for all models, ranging from 0.6% to 5.4%"; GPT-4o: "mean accuracy of 74% (SD 9.4%), with a mean confidence of 63%"; and the paradox: "The correlation between the mean confidence scores for correct answers and model accuracy was inverse and statistically significant (r=−0.40; P=.001)" — "worse-performing models exhibited paradoxically higher confidence" (Qwen2-7B: accuracy 46%, confidence 76%). Recommendations: "human-in-the-loop protocols and the implementation of ensemble methods." **This is the strongest single source that AegisMed's LLM report agent must not supply the user-facing confidence.**

**Sampling-based and token-level confidence.**

- Self-consistency (agreement over multiple samples) is the black-box baseline used inside both [10] (frequency score + self-consistency) and [15] (peer settings). It correlates with correctness better than verbalized confidence but, per [5], "none of these techniques consistently outperform others."
- Token-level (logit) confidence is unavailable on many OpenRouter free-tier endpoints; the black-box result in [10-b]: frequency-based posterior "outperforms logit-based PE" on MedMCQA/MedQA — good news for AegisMed's constraint.
- Conformal wrappers for LLMs exist beyond MCQA (CONFLARE for RAG retrieval uncertainty, arXiv:2404.04287 — relevant to AegisMed's FAISS layer; "Conformal Prediction for LLMs Without Prompt Access," arXiv:2403.01216; Conformal Language Modeling, Quach et al., ICLR 2024). Method citations only; not individually fetched. `WEAK-EVIDENCE`.

**2023→2026 shift:** 2023 established "LLMs are overconfident" [5]; 2024–2025 produced clinical-domain quantification (r=−0.40; 76.1% vs 74.4%) [6]; 2025–2026 moved to *guarantees* — conformal wrappers that meet coverage on MedQA/MedMCQA under black-box access [10-a,10-b] — and to adversarial/multi-agent caveats [15]. A public diagnostic assistant in 2026 is expected to ship calibrated sets or deferral, not raw verbalized confidence.

## 4. Bayesian deep learning / evidential methods for diagnosis

- **Foundations:** Sensoy M, Kaplan L, Kandemir M. "Evidential Deep Learning to Quantify Classification Uncertainty." NeurIPS 2018 (arXiv:1806.01768; fetched via arXiv API). Verbatim: "By placing a Dirichlet distribution on the class probabilities, we treat predictions of a neural net as subjective opinions"; achieves "unprecedented success on detection of out-of-distribution queries and endurance against adversarial perturbations."
- **Survey:** Gao J, et al. "A Comprehensive Survey on Evidential Deep Learning and Its Applications." arXiv:2409.04720 (v1 2024-09-07; fetched via arXiv API): EDL provides reliable uncertainty "in a single forward pass" for high-risk applications including medical diagnosis.
- **Medical benchmarks (imaging):** Tan HS, Wang K, Mcbeth R. "Uncertainty-Error correlations in Evidential Deep Learning models for biomedical segmentation" (arXiv:2410.18461, TAAI 2024; fetched via arXiv API): validates EDL with U-Net backbones on cardiac/prostate MRI (Medical Segmentation Decathlon), finding "superior uncertainty-error correlations vs. Shannon entropy, MC Dropout, and Deep Ensembles," plus active-learning benefits. Ren K, et al. "Uncertainty-informed Mutual Learning for Joint Medical Image Classification and Segmentation" (arXiv:2303.10049; fetched via arXiv API) uses EDL for image-level confidence in joint classification/segmentation.
- **Tabular/symptom-level EDL for diagnosis:** the only direct hits are conference-level (e.g., "Uncertainty-Aware Deep Learning for Reliable Clinical Diagnosis" using tabular data, JIDMIS; class-balanced EDL for health diagnostics, ResearchGate 2024). `WEAK-EVIDENCE` — EDL on symptom-tabular diagnosis is not well established in top venues; treat as exploratory for AegisMed.
- **Practical read for AegisMed:** EDL requires replacing GradientBoosting with a neural evidential head — a research project, not a fix. Split-conformal on the existing GradientBoosting scores (or MC-dropout only if models move to neural nets) achieves distribution-free coverage with ~50 lines of code and no retraining (§2). For the paper, position EDL as future work; for the release, conformal is the shippable method.

## 5. Regulatory angle: uncertainty quantification in CDS

- **FDA CDS guidance (fetched landing page; final guidance January 2026, page current 2026-01-29).** https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software — "This guidance clarifies FDA's thinking on the types of clinical decision support (CDS) software functions that are excluded from the definition of device." Whether AegisMed falls inside or outside device CDS hinges on the four non-device criteria (time-critical basis; acquisition/processing/presentation of medical information; basis-reviewability; intended use), including the statutory transparency requirement that the software enables the health care professional "to independently review the basis for such recommendations" (21 U.S.C. § 520(o)(1)(E) — statutory text capture failed; see UNKNOWN). For a *consumer*-facing assistant with no HCP in the loop, the exclusion is largely unavailable — plan communications accordingly.
- **FDA/Health Canada/MHRA "Transparency for Machine Learning-Enabled Medical Devices: Guiding Principles" (fetched; page current 2024-06-13).** Principles structured as "who (relevant audiences) – why (motivation) – what (relevant information) – where (placement of information) – when (timing) – how (methods used to support transparency)." What-information explicitly includes "confidence intervals associated with the provided outputs" and "known gaps in the data characterization"; "Details about device performance, benefits and risks can help one decide how best to use the device." **This is a direct regulatory expectation that a released tool states output uncertainty — ECE 0.245 without correction does not satisfy it.**
- **Good Machine Learning Practice (fetched FDA page).** Joint FDA/Health Canada/MHRA GMLP guiding principles (October 2021) informed the IMDRF final document (January 2025) "that identifies 10 guiding principles that can inform the development of Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP)." Relevant principle themes: independence of training/test data, reference standards, human-AI team performance, and information provided to users. (Verbatim principle list requires the linked IMDRF PDF — see UNKNOWN.)
- **EU MDR / CE:** uncertainty expectations (clinical evaluation, PMS, Annex XIV/XV) not verified this pass. `UNKNOWN — how to find out: read MDCG 2019-11 (qualification of software as medical device) + MDCG 2020-1; map "uncertainty display" to Annex I GSPR 14/17/20; defer detail to 07_regulatory_pathways.md.`

## Implications for AegisMed

1. **Stop treating `confidence_scorer.py`'s availability/agreement blend as a probability — replace it with a conformal wrapper.** Concretely: split the (rebuilt, non-circular) eval data into train/calibration/test; implement APS or RAPS over the 15 conditions (src/agents/confidence_scorer.py, plus a new src/core/conformal.py) so every diagnosis ships as a prediction set at a stated α (0.10 for triage, 0.05 for emergencies). The blend may remain as a *feature* inside the score, but the user-facing number must come from calibrated quantiles.
2. **Conformalize before blending, and audit the blend.** Per [15], blending agreement *after* calibration destroys coverage (90%→74%). If agent agreement stays in the pipeline, re-fit calibration on the *final blended score* (so the guarantee covers the whole stack), or calibrate each agent's evidence-slice score separately and aggregate only inside the set-construction step. Add a regression test that measures empirical coverage of the shipped pipeline, not just the base classifier.
3. **Add RCPS-style FNR control to the emergency triage band.** The emergency band is a costly-mistake decision: use Bates et al.'s finite-sample risk control [9] to set the emergency-band threshold so that, on held-out data, FNR ≤ a chosen level (e.g., 5%) with stated confidence. This is a stronger, patent/paper-friendly safety claim than "we show confidence."
4. **Never display LLM verbalized confidence.** Report agent (src/agents/synthesis/agent.py) must not emit "I am 90% sure." Evidence: right-vs-wrong confidence differs by 0.6–5.4 pp and accuracy-confidence correlation is inverse (r=−0.40) [6]; verbalized confidence is overconfident in professional-knowledge tasks [5]. Display: calibrated set + triage band + guideline citations instead.
5. **Use frequency-based black-box conformality for OpenRouter free-tier models.** With no logits, adopt the Yang & Liu frequency-based non-conformity score [10-b] and Ke et al.'s self-consistency scoring [10-a]: sample k responses, score by answer frequency, conformalize. This fits the black-box constraint and is published on MedQA/MedMCQA.
6. **Per-condition calibration, not just global ECE.** Myers et al. show dataset-level calibration hides subgroup overconfidence [1]. Report ECE per condition and per triage band in the eval suite; flag any condition whose calibrated set is systematically empty or oversized. Target: bring ECE 0.245 to <0.05 via temperature/isotonic calibration on held-out data, then verify coverage empirically at α=0.10.
7. **Conformal (CQR-style) intervals for the GradientBoosting risk models — and retire synthetic-only training claims.** Romano CQR [source §2] gives distribution-free risk intervals; but with 500 synthetic records the exchangeability assumption to real patients is untestable, so the report must state "coverage guaranteed under the synthetic distribution; real-world shift unknown" — matching the FDA transparency principle of disclosing "known gaps in the data characterization" [12].
8. **Add explicit abstention/deferral.** Tian et al.'s definition: calibrated confidence exists to enable "deferral to an expert in cases of low-confidence predictions" [7]. UI rule: if the conformal set at α=0.10 has >3 conditions or contains an emergency condition, the report leads with "seek in-person care" and suppresses the differential. This is both the safest and the regulator-aligned behavior [11,12].
9. **Paper positioning (CHIL):** the uncertainty section is AegisMed's most defensible contribution — "distribution-free conformal triage over an entropy-driven active-questioning pipeline, evaluated under symptom-exposure degradation" — because it combines §2 guarantees with the interactive setting, and cites [10,15] as the methodological hooks. Frame the current ECE 0.245 as the *motivation*, with the conformal pipeline as the fix, and report empirical vs nominal coverage on every table.
